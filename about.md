## Mission

Zabanestan is a non-profit volunteer-based group that documents, preserves, and teaches endangered and vulnerable Indo-Iranian languages, and makes what it produces freely available. We collect and synthesize resources from around the world, develop open-source software and tools tailored to learning the languages and dialects we seek to preserve, provide general guidance for acquiring rare languages, and work with native speakers and heritage communities.

## Scope

Our preservation work centers on a specific inventory of endangered and vulnerable languages by priority, [published on our website](languages.html) with sources. To qualify in our list of priority languages, a language must be in the Indo-Iranian language family and considered endangered or vulnerable by one of our approved sources (UNESCO, Glottolog, or Encyclopaedia Iranica) or reasonable evidence provided by peer-reviewed articles. Dialects of Persian may also count, provided there is enough cultural or linguistic uniqueness to warrant their consideration, and they are deemed endangered or vulnerable.

Our long-term scope is the whole Indo-Iranian family: the Iranic, Indo-Aryan, and Nuristani branches. We are starting with the Iranic branch and will add languages from the other branches as we gain volunteers, speakers, and sources.

Our priority list considers the following factors, in order of importance:

1. Endangerment level, based on UNESCO, Glottolog, and Encyclopaedia Iranica.
2. Within each level, resource scarcity.
3. The number of available teachers, community contributions, and the ability of our independent research volunteers to acquire reliable, high-quality resources.

Languages of the wider Persianate world that don’t meet the endangerment criteria (for instance: Turkic languages, stable Persian dialects, and extinct languages) are not prioritized as major outputs on our public platforms. Changes to either list are made only by the core team, with sources.

## What we make

We produce five kinds of work, and every finished piece is published somewhere durable, not only on Discord.

- **Learning materials:** lessons, vocabulary decks, learning software, and bilingual readers written by our team and openly licensed.
- **Reference resources:** bibliographies, word lists, and dictionaries built from several sources and checked with speakers.
- **Documentation:** recordings of speakers with transcriptions and translations, made with consent and deposited in a recognized archive.
- **Translation projects:** classical and modern texts brought into English.
- **Community:** classes, study groups, and social media, where members learn, practice, and discuss together.

Finished work goes to our website, our Discord server, lasting platforms such as a language archive, Living Dictionaries, and Wikimedia projects, or a private drive for submissions by users who ask us to archive their own language material without publicly sharing.

## Principles

Six commitments guide every decision we make.

- **Speakers first.** Native speakers and heritage communities inform the way we make decisions about their own languages and how they are credited for their work.
- **Accuracy.** We always cite reliable sources, and corrections from the community are welcome. We strive to create a free and self-correcting dialogue in order to maintain the highest degree of trust.
- **Open by default.** What we create is free to use and share, unless a speaker asks for limits on their own material.
- **Legal compliance.** We cite our sources and don’t redistribute anyone’s copyrighted work without permission.
- **Durability.** We aim to make priority languages possible to learn for generations to come.
- **Nonpartisanship.** Zabanestan is a cultural and educational project and doesn’t take part in political advocacy.

## Publishing and rights

We publish only what we have the right to share: our own work, public-domain works, openly licensed works, and material that we have obtained written permission to use.

- **Our license.** Original materials are released under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), the license Wikimedia projects use for text ([Wikimedia terms](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_use/Creative_Commons_4.0)), so our work can flow into Wikipedia and Wiktionary.
- **Software.** Code we write is released under the [MIT License](https://opensource.org/license/mit).
- **Contributions.** Contributors keep the copyright to their own work. By submitting original work for publication, they agree to release it under the licenses above. Speaker recordings follow the terms each speaker chose when giving consent, not these default licenses.
- **Other people’s books and articles.** We cite them and link to legitimate copies: the publisher, the author, a library, or an open repository. We don’t post scans or files without permission, and we don’t link to pirate sites.
- **Building our own materials.** We draw from and cite sources (grammar, vocabulary, sound systems), synthesizing everything in our own words, with our own examples. We don’t copy anyone’s explanations, example sentences, exercises, dialogues, or the selection and order of their dictionary entries without permission.
- **Rights log.** Every public item records its source, license, contributors, and (if applicable) consent status. Nothing goes on a public channel without a rights status.
- **Takedowns.** Anyone can ask us to remove their own material. We will review, remove, and reply within 7 days.

## Consent and speaker safety guidelines

No one is recorded, quoted, or credited without [informed consent]({{ site.consent_form }}).

- **Before recording,** we explain what will be recorded, where it will be stored and published, and who can access it. Consent is given in writing or recorded aloud.
- **Credit** is the speaker’s choice: full name, pseudonym, or anonymous.
- **Access** is the speaker’s choice too: open, restricted (for example, researchers or community members only), or private.
- **Withdrawal.** Speakers can withdraw consent at any time. We remove the material from our platforms and ask any archive holding it to restrict access under its own rules.
- **Minors** are recorded only with a parent’s or guardian’s consent.
- **Higher-risk contributors.** For anyone in Iran, or anywhere minority-language activity carries risk, anonymity is the default. We show no faces, names, or locations unless they explicitly choose otherwise, and we strip location data from files.
- **No political framing.** We never attach political content or commentary to a speaker’s contribution.
- **Records.** Consent records and private submissions are kept in restricted folders that only the Coordinator, the Archivist, and the volunteer who collected the material can open. They are kept for as long as we hold the material they cover.

## Conduct

Everyone who takes part in Zabanestan, including members, volunteers, and speakers, is treated with respect regardless of background, dialect, or level. Harassment, hate speech, and personal attacks are not tolerated, and neither is pressuring a speaker to share more than they have agreed to.

## First-year priorities

Two things come first, in this order:

1. **Flagship output.** Finish one durable output for one high-priority language that has a speaker collaborator.
2. **Language leads.** Recruit a lead for each of the top-ranked languages on the core roster.
